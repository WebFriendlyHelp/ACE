# Accessibility

ACE exists because reading iPhone messages with a screen reader was miserable. It's made by Web Friendly Help LLC, an assistive technology business, and built and tested by a blind developer.

## Supported environments

- Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit.
- NVDA, JAWS, and Narrator.
- Your own Windows font size, colors, and high contrast settings. ACE follows them rather than overriding them.

## What ACE does for screen reader and keyboard users

- A two-pane window: the list of conversations, and the transcript of the one you're reading.
- Moving through a transcript by message, speaker, day, month, year, or attachment, so a long conversation isn't one wall of text.
- An action menu on every message, so copying it or saving its attachment doesn't take hunting.
- Calls and voicemails on their own tabs, reachable with Control plus 1 through Control plus 4.
- Voicemail transcripts shown as text, which is the only part of a voicemail a deaf-blind reader can reach.
- Exports to Word, plain text, or CSV.

WCAG 2.2 AA is the bar I test against where its criteria apply to a desktop program. That's a working standard, not a formal certification.

## Known barriers

None are documented yet. ACE is new, and I'd rather list real ones you've hit than guess. When reports come in, they'll be listed here.

## Reporting a barrier

Open an issue on the ACE issue tracker. Say which screen reader and version you use, what you were trying to do, and what happened instead. Please leave out the content of your messages; a description of the problem is enough.

Or email help@webfriendlyhelp.com if you'd rather not post publicly.
